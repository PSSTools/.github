# psstools Corporate Contributor License Agreement

**Version 1.0 — 2026-09-22**

> **When this is the right document.** If contributions are made as part of employment, the
> employer — not the individual — most likely owns the copyright, and an
> [ICLA](ICLA.md) signed by the individual alone does not cover it. This Agreement is signed
> once by the company and then covers every employee it lists, so each new contributor does
> not restart the legal conversation.
>
> The PSS tooling user base is largely corporate. This document exists so that the first
> serious contribution from a company does not stall for six months.

---

## What this is, and what it is not

This is the Apache Software Foundation's Corporate Contributor License Agreement v1.0, with
the counterparty changed and **one section added**. It is a *license*, not an assignment —
the Corporation keeps the copyright in everything its employees write, and may use those
contributions for any other purpose.

**The differences from the stock Apache CCLA v1.0**, so counsel can diff quickly:

1. **The counterparty is a person, not a foundation.** "the Foundation" is replaced
   throughout by "the Maintainer", defined below as Matthew Ballance. psstools is a GitHub
   organization — a namespace, not a legal person, and so not capable of being a party.
2. **Section 9 is new** — stewardship, relicensing, and assignment. It is identical in
   substance to section 9 of the [ICLA](ICLA.md), and the reason it exists is stated inside
   it.
3. The stock "Software Grant" preamble, covering an initial one-off transfer of an existing
   codebase, is retained as **Schedule B** and is optional — leave it blank if this Agreement
   covers ongoing contributions only.
4. Sections 1–8 are otherwise **unchanged** apart from the counterparty substitution.

Nothing in this document is legal advice.

### 9. Stewardship, relicensing, and assignment

*This section is not part of the stock Apache CCLA. It is identical in substance to section 9
of the [ICLA](ICLA.md).*

**(a) Relicensing.** You agree that the Maintainer may license Your Contributions, and works
derived from them, under any license approved by the Open Source Initiative, in addition to
or in place of the license under which they were originally distributed.

**(b) Stewardship.** You agree that the Maintainer may contribute Your Contributions to a
standards organization — such as Accellera Systems Initiative, which publishes the Portable
Test and Stimulus Standard these tools implement — or to a foundation or other entity that
undertakes long-term stewardship of the Work, under that organization's contribution terms.

**(c) Assignment.** The Maintainer may assign this Agreement, together with the rights
granted under it, to a successor — including an entity formed by the Maintainer to hold the
Work, or an organization assuming stewardship of it. Upon assignment this Agreement continues
in force unchanged and the successor becomes the Maintainer for all purposes of this
Agreement. **No further action by You is required**, and You will not be asked to sign again.

**(d) What this does not permit.** Nothing in this section allows the Maintainer to withdraw
any version of the Work that has already been distributed under the Apache License 2.0. That
license is irrevocable by its own terms; this Agreement neither takes back nor can take back
what has already been released under it. Your rights in Your own Contributions are
unaffected — You retain full ownership and may license or use them however You wish,
including in proprietary work.

**Why it exists.** PSS is an Accellera standard. The best plausible outcome for a tool like
`pssparser` or `pssc` is that it outlives its current maintainer under a standards body or a
foundation. Apache-2.0 plus sign-off does not make that possible without tracking down every
contributor individually, years after the fact, which in practice is how such moves die.
Paragraphs (a)–(c) keep that door open; paragraph (d) is what keeps the door from swinging
the other way.
